CONSTRUCTION & CONTRACTOR PERMITS - BOSTON
For contractors, builders, and construction companies

=== COMMON CONSTRUCTION PERMITS ===

1. HOT WORK PERMIT
   - For: Welding, cutting, brazing, or any fire-producing work
   - Apply: Fire Prevention Portal
   - Contact: Fire Prevention 617-343-3628
   - Duration: Typically short-term or single job
   - Requirements: Fire watch, fire extinguisher on site, clear area
   - Common issues: Forgetting to get permit before starting, not having fire safety equipment
   - Timeline: Usually quick approval (same day to 1-2 days)

2. TEMPORARY DUMPSTER
   - For: Construction dumpsters on street or property
   - Apply: Fire Prevention Portal
   - Duration: Specified time period
   - Requirements: Location that doesn't block fire access
   - Renewal: Can extend if needed
   - Common issues: Placement blocking hydrants or emergency access, overfilling

3. CONSTRUCTION FIRE SAFETY
   - For: Active construction sites
   - Apply: Fire Prevention Portal
   - Requirements: Fire extinguishers, access for emergency vehicles, hot work compliance
   - Ongoing: Throughout construction period
   - Inspections: May have periodic checks

4. TEMPORARY ELECTRICAL SERVICE
   - For: Construction site power before permanent service
   - Apply: ISD Portal (licensed electrician applies)
   - Examples: Job site trailers, temporary lighting, power tools
   - Must: Meet same safety standards as permanent
   - Common issues: Proper grounding, weatherproof equipment, overloading circuits

5. SCAFFOLDING & STAGING
   - For: Exterior scaffolding on buildings
   - Requirements: Sidewalk protection, proper engineering for large scaffolds
   - May need: Street occupancy permit if encroaching on sidewalk
   - Safety: Regular inspections required, fall protection

=== WORKING IN BOSTON ===

Contractor License Requirements:
- Massachusetts Construction Supervisor License (CSL) for contractors
- Specific trade licenses: Electrician (required), Plumber (required), Gas Fitter (required)
- Home Improvement Contractor registration
- Proof of insurance required for all permits

Worker Requirements:
- OSHA certifications for certain work
- Proper safety equipment
- Current photo ID on job sites

=== STREET & SIDEWALK WORK ===

Street Occupancy Permit:
- For: Work that encroaches on street or sidewalk
- Examples: Scaffolding, dumpsters, material storage, equipment
- Contact: Public Works Department
- Requirements: Traffic control plan, insurance, signage
- Fees: Based on area and duration
- Common issues: Blocking sidewalk accessibility, not maintaining safe pedestrian path

Excavation Permit:
- For: Digging in streets, sidewalks, or public property
- Examples: Utility work, foundation work affecting public way
- Requirements: Restoration plan, traffic control, dig safe notifications
- Must call: Dig Safe (811) before excavating

=== COORDINATION & TIMING ===

Multiple Permits for One Job:
Most construction projects need several permits:
- Building permit (main structural work)
- Electrical permit (wiring, panels, service)
- Plumbing permit (pipes, fixtures)
- Gas permit (gas lines)
- Hot work permit (welding/cutting)
- Dumpster permit (waste management)
- Street occupancy (if needed)

Inspection Sequencing:
1. Start work only after permits obtained
2. Rough inspections (before covering work): 
   - Framing
   - Electrical rough
   - Plumbing rough
   - Gas rough
3. Insulation inspection (before covering)
4. Final inspections (all trades)
5. Certificate of occupancy (if required)

Critical: Don't cover up rough work before inspection!

=== COMMON CONTRACTOR ISSUES ===

1. Starting Before Permits
   - Problem: Work done before permit issued
   - Result: Stop work order, fines, may need to expose work for inspection
   - Solution: Always wait for permit approval

2. Missing Inspections
   - Problem: Covered up work without calling for rough inspection
   - Result: May need to open walls to inspect, major delays
   - Solution: Call for inspections before covering ANY work

3. Failed Inspections
   - Problem: Work doesn't meet code
   - Result: Stop work order, must fix issues, re-inspection required
   - Common failures: Improper framing, electrical code violations, plumbing issues
   - Solution: Know current Boston building codes, hire qualified workers

4. Wrong Permit Type
   - Problem: Applied for short form when long form needed (or vice versa)
   - Result: Permit rejection, restart application process
   - Solution: Call ISD first to verify correct permit type

5. License & Insurance Issues
   - Problem: Expired license or lack of insurance
   - Result: Can't pull permits, legal liability
   - Solution: Keep all licenses and insurance current, have proof ready

=== WORKING IN HISTORIC DISTRICTS ===

Additional Requirements:
- Landmarks Commission approval needed
- Exterior work has design restrictions
- Takes additional time (add 4-8 weeks)
- More documentation required
- Even minor changes (windows, doors, siding) need approval

Boston Historic Districts:
- Beacon Hill, Back Bay, South End, and others
- Check if property is in historic district before planning work
- Contact Landmarks Commission early in process

=== EMERGENCY WORK ===

When Permit Can't Wait:
- Life safety issues (structural collapse, fire damage)
- Emergency repairs (burst pipes, electrical hazards)

Process:
- Make emergency safe first
- Contact ISD immediately
- Get permit after emergency stabilization
- May still need to expose work for inspection

=== BEST PRACTICES ===

Before Starting:
1. Verify ALL required permits for the job
2. Check contractor licenses are current
3. Confirm insurance certificates
4. Pull permits BEFORE ordering materials or starting work
5. Post permits visibly at job site
6. Have emergency contact available

During Construction:
1. Keep permits posted at site
2. Schedule inspections in advance (24-48 hours notice)
3. Don't cover work before inspection
4. Document progress with photos
5. Keep job site safe and clean
6. Respect noise ordinances (typically 7am-6pm weekdays)

After Work:
1. Call for final inspections
2. Get signed off permits
3. Provide copies to property owner
4. Remove dumpsters and clean site
5. Close out permits properly

=== TIMELINE PLANNING ===

Typical Permit Timelines:
- Hot work permit: Same day to 2 days
- Dumpster permit: 1-3 days
- Short form building permit: 1-2 weeks
- Long form building permit: 2-6 weeks
- Complex projects: 8-12+ weeks
- Trade permits (electrical/plumbing/gas): 1-3 days to 1 week

Add Buffer Time:
- Always add 2-4 weeks buffer to timeline
- Inspections can delay if failed
- Weather delays
- Material delivery delays
- Other trades' coordination

=== VIOLATIONS & PENALTIES ===

Common Violations:
- Working without permit
- Work not matching approved plans
- Missing inspections
- Unsafe conditions
- Blocking fire access
- Noise violations

Consequences:
- Stop work orders
- Fines ($100-$1000+ per day)
- Must fix violations
- Potential license suspension
- Legal liability if injury occurs

=== HELPFUL TIPS ===

- Build relationship with ISD and Fire Prevention staff
- Call ahead with questions rather than assuming
- Keep copies of all permits and approvals
- Take photos before, during, and after work
- Communicate with property owners about permit requirements
- Schedule inspections as early as possible
- Have backup plans for inspection failures
- Know current year's building code amendments
- Join contractor associations for support and updates
